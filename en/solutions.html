Solutions: Foreign Trade & Cross-Border Commerce

Your pain points, broken down

The lead-gen and sales challenges foreign trade teams face break down into a few specific scenarios:

Scenario 1: A single overseas acquisition channel, with rising costs

现状

The team relies heavily on trade shows and Alibaba.com for leads. Shows run 2-3 times a year at tens of thousands each; Alibaba inquiry quality is uneven with heavy same-spec competition. You want English SEO and LinkedIn content marketing, but nobody on the team can consistently produce professional English content.

SiliMind 怎么解决

Type your product keywords into the AI topic center and get 10 topics scored with search volume and competition. Pick one, and AI generates an English SEO-optimized draft in 30 seconds — based on the keyword and target audience. A built-in SEO check panel sits in the editor; approved content goes out through the unified distribution gateway to third-party distribution services covering platforms at home and abroad.

The attribution dashboard shows which article drove how many inquiries and deals — ROI at a glance. Unlike trade shows and Alibaba, where money goes out and results run on gut feeling.

Scenario 2: Customer messages scattered across multiple apps

现状

Overseas customers ask prices on WhatsApp, send POs by email, and occasionally engage on LinkedIn. Domestic customers use WeChat, phone, and WeCom. Reps switch between 4-5 apps daily; what customers asked relies entirely on memory. When a rep leaves, customer information leaves with them.

SiliMind 怎么解决

WhatsApp, Email, Telegram, Instagram, Messenger, and LinkedIn messages auto-aggregate onto the customer 360° profile's "sales touchpoint" timeline; phone calls, memos, WeChat, and WeCom interactions can also be logged manually on the same timeline. See every interaction on one timeline, with sentiment tags (whether recent customer communication leans positive or negative). A new rep reads the timeline for 5 minutes and is ready to go.

Scenario 3: Inconsistent quality in technical answers

现状

Customers ask about product specs, certification standards, and installation on WhatsApp or WeChat. Answer quality varies — sometimes the answer is wrong and the customer goes straight to a competitor.

SiliMind 怎么解决

Upload product spec sheets to the knowledge base (PDF/Word/Markdown/URL) and AI indexes them automatically. When a customer message contains a technical question, an "AI suggested reply" appears beside it — AI drafts the reply from the knowledge base with cited sources ("Ref: Spec Sheet V3.2, p.12"). Reps confirm before sending; the semi-automatic mode guarantees accuracy.

Scenario 4: The boss can't see real-time business data

现状

Business data sits in the Alibaba backend, Excel sheets, and reps' personal notes. When the boss wants inquiry volume, conversion rates, or deal distribution by product line, they wait for finance's monthly report.

SiliMind 怎么解决

The management dashboard shows company-wide metrics in real time — domestic and export data on one platform. ChatBI natural-language queries: "Deal value distribution for North America this month", "Compare marketing spend vs. deal value between domestic East China and export North America". AI proactively alerts: a high-value opportunity stalling, lead volume down month-over-month, a rep's response speed below average.

Scenario 5: Key-account follow-up by hand in domestic sales

现状

Many foreign trade teams also sell domestically. Domestic customers communicate on WeChat with frequent visits (on-site + phone), and contracts differ from export (RMB settlement, VAT invoices, payment terms). Two customer management systems run in parallel; reps switch back and forth.

SiliMind 怎么解决

Domestic and export customers are managed in the same CRM, with WeChat and WhatsApp messages converging on one timeline. Visit management covers on-site visits, phone calls, trade shows, and online meetings, with AI generating structured notes automatically. Contract management supports multi-currency quotes (RMB/USD) with a unified approval flow.
